# SIGNAL functional audit — 27 October 2026

## Verdict

SIGNAL has substantial content and a working application shell, but its daily workflows are not yet dependable enough to treat its plans, shopping totals and saved progress as authoritative. The biggest problems are inconsistent data contracts and separate implementations of the same journey, rather than visual polish.

Priorities follow the owner's goal: reliable personal use of training and NZ meal planning first, optional cycle context, then paid membership and local community.

## Fix before wider testing

### 1. Private data and meal plans are not isolated by account

**Priority: P1 — confirmed source defect; live cross-account test outstanding.**

`useJournalSync` loads the device-wide journal cache, merges local entries with the current user's cloud entries, and attempts to upload local-only entries under the current user's ID. The journal and vault caches contain no user ID. Signing out does not clear these stores. A second person using the browser can therefore see the previous person's local entries in the merged UI; whether an attempted cloud upsert succeeds also depends on deployed database policies and whether that entry already exists remotely.

Custom meal plans also use shared keys and timestamps. If the local timestamp is newer, signing in to another account can upload that device's previous meal plan into the new account. Ordinary training path selection/completion and cycle caches have similar ownership gaps.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/journal-store.ts:130`, `src/hooks/useJournalSync.ts:109`, `src/hooks/useJournalSync.ts:119`, `src/hooks/useCustomMealPlan.ts:20`, `src/hooks/useCustomMealPlan.ts:104`, `src/pages/Account.tsx:180`.

**Fix:** namespace private caches by user, reset in-memory state and queries on account changes, bind queued writes to the originating account, and explicitly handle guest-to-member migration. Verify A → sign out → B and delayed writes during switching.

### 2. Meal generation can ignore dietary exclusions

**Priority: P1 — reproduced with actual recipe data.**

A 28-day generated plan with `allergies: 'almond'` still contains **20 almond-containing meals/snacks**. Snack templates never receive dietary preferences. Adult recipe selection explicitly discards dietary/avoidance filters when the filtered pool is empty; child meal selection also falls back to an unfiltered protein match.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/build-db-meal-plan.ts:91`, `:137`, `:228`, `:300`; probe evidence contains concrete almond examples.

**Fix:** make exclusions mandatory across meals, snacks, child alternatives and replacements. If no suitable recipe exists, show that gap and request a different choice instead of silently relaxing exclusions. Test empty candidate pools as well as common combinations.

### 3. Onboarding saves a meal-plan format that Eat cannot read

**Priority: P1 — reproduced by executing the consumer against the producer's saved shape.**

Onboarding writes `{ plan: [...], generatedAt, cycleDay }` to `signal_meal_plan`. The current meal readers expect `{ days: [...], prepPreferences, ... }`. `getAIMealPlan` accepts either object without validation; `getTodayMeals` immediately calls `.days.find`. The probe throws **“Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'find')”**. This is conditional on the onboarding generation succeeding, not an assertion that the live edge function always succeeds.

**Evidence:** `src/components/OnboardingFlow.tsx:478`, `src/lib/weekly-planner.ts:469`, `src/lib/today-meals.ts:69`, `src/lib/build-custom-shopping-plan.ts:96`.

**Fix:** one versioned meal-plan schema, validation and migration at the storage boundary, and an onboarding-to-home-to-Eat integration test.

## Make Eat usable for a real week

### 4. Planned meals can have no recipe and contribute no groceries

**Priority: P1 — reproduced in browser and source probes.**

The active recipe index contains **324 recipes**, all with nonempty ingredients/methods. The problem is connecting the plan to those recipes. Across a default 28-day cycle's 84 main-meal slots, **31 Today slots fail recipe lookup**. Shopping uses a slightly different name-cleaning path and fails for **28 slots**, replacing them with empty ingredient/method arrays.

In the browser, day-8 breakfast “Green smoothie — spinach, apple, ginger, flaxseed” offered View recipe, but expanded to no ingredients or method. It nevertheless displayed 380 calories and 22 g protein from the generic fallback. Shopping silently omitted breakfast ingredients. Fuzzy name matching can also choose a different dish without an explicit substitution.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/today-meals.ts:87`, `src/lib/recipe-index.ts:82`, `src/lib/build-custom-shopping-plan.ts:54`, `:63`, `src/components/nutrition/TodayTab.tsx:58`, `:490`.

**Fix:** use stable recipe IDs throughout planning; validate every slot. Unresolved meals must be visible errors or explicit substitutions, and missing nutrition must be shown as unknown rather than generic numbers.

### 5. Shopping omits leftovers' extra portions, separate children's meals and default snacks

**Priority: P1 — source-confirmed, leftover generation reproduced.**

- Double-dinner planning copies dinner into tomorrow's lunch and marks it as a leftover. Shopping skips that lunch, but dinner quantities are not doubled. In the probe, two adults eating the lentil soup at dinner and next lunch require four servings; the aggregation includes only the dinner's two servings.
- The shopping loop includes adult meals/snacks only; it never consumes `kidsLunch` or `kidsDinner`. Multiplying adult meals by 0.6 per child does not purchase the ingredients for a different children's recipe.
- Without a generated plan, the shopping builder supplies empty snack placeholders, while Today displays specific snacks. With a generated plan, Today still uses its own separate snack templates, which may differ from the groceries generated for the plan.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/build-db-meal-plan.ts:321`, `src/components/nutrition/SmartShoppingList.tsx:165`, `:168`, `:173`, `src/lib/build-custom-shopping-plan.ts:69`, `src/components/nutrition/TodayTab.tsx:121`.

**Fix:** represent cooking batches, portions consumed, carried leftovers and child alternatives in the shared plan. Derive shopping and Today from that same data, including cross-week leftovers.

### 6. Portion controls do not consistently change what is cooked or bought

**Priority: P1 — source-confirmed.**

Today's portion buttons scale displayed/logged macros, while the ingredients and method remain the original recipe text. Separately, the List tab defaults to one adult/zero kids using `signal_shopping_portions`; the Plan's shopping view uses its own household preferences. They can produce different totals for the same meals.

**Evidence:** `src/components/nutrition/TodayTab.tsx:490` onward, `src/components/nutrition/WeekShoppingList.tsx:16`, `src/components/nutrition/MyWeekTab.tsx:435`.

**Fix:** define whether a portion means recipe multiplier or servings per person, use a shared household setting, and scale ingredient quantities with the displayed portions. Keep both list entry points consistent.

### 7. Ingredient parsing still produces unreliable shopping rows

**Priority: P2 — reproduced.**

Examples from the parser/aggregation probes:

- `2 x 400g tins chickpeas` becomes quantity `2`, no unit, name `x 400g tins chickpeas`.
- `400g tinned chopped tomatoes` becomes name `g tinned chopped tomatoes` because preparation-word removal strips the meaningful noun and the fallback retains the unit.
- One cup dried chickpeas plus one cup cooked chickpeas merges into `Dried chickpeas — 500 ml`, losing the distinction between ingredient forms.

Browser shopping also showed `Salt and pepper — 7`, pumpkin seeds under Produce, and seeds measured in ml. This is not yet the clear supermarket list the owner needs.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/ingredient-parser.ts:25` onward, `src/lib/smart-shopping-core.ts:176` onward; evidence JSON.

**Fix:** structured quantity/unit/ingredient/form fields; preserve dry/cooked/canned distinctions and pack sizes; retain original strings when parsing is uncertain. Do not invent mass conversions where density is unknown. Validate against the actual NZ recipe catalogue.

### 8. Cloud restore can select custom picks instead of the generated meal plan

**Priority: P2 — source-confirmed conditional failure.**

Both generated plans and custom-pick documents use `plan_type = nutrition`. MyWeekTab fetches only the newest nutrition row, then rejects it if it has no `days`. When the custom-pick document is newest, a valid older generated plan is never loaded on a fresh device.

**Evidence:** `src/components/nutrition/MyWeekTab.tsx:143`, `src/hooks/useCustomMealPlan.ts:23`.

**Fix:** distinguish document types in the query/schema and restore each independently. Test a generated plan followed by a manual recipe pick, then a fresh-device restore.

## Make training dependable

### 9. Ordinary training completion does not restore from the cloud

**Priority: P1 — source-confirmed; cloud round-trip test outstanding.**

Non-partner completion is stored only in `signal_path_completed_sessions`. A separate fire-and-forget insert writes a calendar log but never restores completion into the local progress map. Returned insert errors are ignored. Clearing storage or changing devices therefore loses the information used to select the next session, even if some workout history exists remotely. Selected-path hydration also runs only from TrainingTab and skips cloud restore whenever any local path already exists.

**Evidence:** `src/lib/training-path-utils.ts:52`, `:81`, `:94`, `:130`, `:162`; `src/components/movement/TrainingTab.tsx`.

**Fix:** account-scoped canonical training progress, durable queued writes, cloud hydration at app entry, and explicit saved/offline/failed status. Test completing a session offline and restoring on another device.

### 10. Non-partner training is mostly instructions plus a completion button

**Priority: P2 — product gap confirmed by code and training preview.**

The strength path has useful exercise prescriptions, but its Today card's Finish workout action simply marks the session complete. The resulting log stores exercise names and the planned duration, with no actual set/repetition/load capture in this path. Library-added picks shown on the card are also absent from `logSessionToCalendar`, which reads only the original session structure. This limits meaningful progression and makes the ordinary paths substantially less complete than the partner class flow.

**Evidence:** `src/components/movement/SelectedPathTodayCard.tsx:154`, `src/lib/training-path-utils.ts:146`, `:162`.

**Fix:** agree a minimal usable session experience for ordinary plans: exercise-by-exercise completion, usable prescribed loads, optional actual performance, and logs that reflect what was done. Preserve the established partner-class prescribed-load workflow.

### 11. Onboarding training generation is disconnected from the visible Today path

**Priority: P2 — source-confirmed integration gap.**

Onboarding invokes the old `generate-plan` flow. The active homepage Today board and Move Today card obtain their workout from `getSelectedPath`, which reads a different static-path selection. Onboarding does not set that selection. Its promise of a ready plan can therefore lead to “Pick a path to begin.” The older AI training screen is not the current TrainingTab.

**Evidence:** `src/components/OnboardingFlow.tsx:416`, `src/components/home/TodayBoard.tsx:80`, `src/components/movement/SelectedPathTodayCard.tsx:101`, `src/components/movement/AITrainingPlanTab.tsx:1`.

**Fix:** make onboarding choose or create the same plan entity consumed by Today, Move and progress tracking. Show the actual first session before calling setup complete.

## Align with the product direction

### 12. Cycle awareness is mandatory in behavior and ignores configured length

**Priority: P1 for planning correctness; product mismatch.**

CycleContext hard-codes modulo 28 and fixed phase boundaries. It does not consume `cycle_length` or `cycle_mode`, even though onboarding saves them. Without a period date, every queried calendar date returns day 8. The browser showed “Follicular · day 8” with no cycle entered; a week's shopping repeated that same day's meal choices. This is especially unsuitable for members opting out of cycle tracking.

**Evidence:** `src/contexts/CycleContext.tsx:49`, `:57`, `:73`, `:159`; `src/components/OnboardingFlow.tsx:379` onward.

**Fix:** make calendar dates the planning foundation and cycle context optional. Respect stored cycle settings, handle dates before the latest period without negative remainder artifacts, and avoid assigning a biological phase when no tracking data exists.

### 13. The intended first-ten-minutes journey is absent

**Priority: P2 — requirement gap.**

The current eight-step flow asks for name, body measurements, cycle status, movement goals, diet, fitness level and equipment. It contains no “My Dream Life looks like…” entry or reflection-to-goals/habits step. Name/body/cycle requirements also precede the value the owner wants people to experience.

**Evidence:** `src/components/OnboardingFlow.tsx:26`, `:518`, `:565` onward.

**Fix:** after the daily flows are stable, reshape onboarding around the owner's intended journal entry and member-reviewed goals, with optional measurements and cycle tracking.

### 14. Nearby community presents random online presence

**Priority: P2 before community beta — source-confirmed.**

NearbyView assigns `is_online: Math.random() > 0.4`. It is a placeholder, not actual presence. This should not be presented as a real person's availability.

**Evidence:** `src/components/community/NearbyView.tsx:473`.

**Fix:** omit the badge until real presence is implemented. A separate authenticated community audit should cover invitations, local discovery, location privacy, blocking/reporting and chat reliability before launch.

### 15. Performance, error recovery and build hygiene need a second pass

**Priority: P2/P3.**

The successful production build emitted an initial JS chunk of roughly **2.84 MB / 794 KB gzip**, plus a Nutrition chunk of **1.06 MB / 298 KB gzip**. This is a meaningful mobile payload, though network performance was not measured. The top-level error fallback is just “Something went wrong,” without recovery controls. Lint identifies conditional React hooks on the BrandGuidelines route and extensive suppressed/weakly typed boundaries.

**Evidence:** build output; `src/main.tsx`; `src/pages/BrandGuidelines.tsx:123`.

**Fix:** repair hook-order defects, add useful retry/recovery states, split large content libraries away from the initial route, and enforce checks for new changes. Do not let general lint cleanup displace the functional blockers above.

## Suggested repair sequence

1. Account isolation, dietary exclusions and plan-schema validation.
2. One recipe/meal-plan model shared by onboarding, Today, Plan, List and saved cloud data.
3. Correct recipe resolution, household portions, leftovers, snacks and child-meal shopping.
4. Restore and save training progress reliably; connect onboarding to the visible training path.
5. Run a real seven-day personal-use acceptance test: plan meals, shop manually through the supermarket link, cook them, complete workouts, reload, go offline/reconnect and switch devices.
6. Then address onboarding narrative, deeper courses, community and performance polish.

Direct supermarket-cart transfer remains a future aspiration. The current manual shopping workflow is a valid testing target; its list must first be complete and trustworthy.

### Follow-up: older training history recovery

Ordinary training now rebuilds completion ticks from valid session markers in the signed-in member's completed workout logs when no progress snapshot or local edit timestamp exists. History is paginated; older untimestamped device completions are carried forward into the new cloud snapshot. Local/remote resets take precedence, failed reads remain retryable, and an account change during the read prevents cache writes. This closes the older-history gap in finding 9; it does not claim conflict-free simultaneous edits across devices.

Validation: 8 new mocked-cloud regressions cover recovery, legacy device progress, local/remote resets, account switching, read errors, pagination and an in-flight reset. Against isolated merged PR12 source, all 35 tests across training recovery, shopping preferences, connected planning and shopping units pass. Live cross-device cloud round trip remains untested.
