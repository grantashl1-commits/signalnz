## Historical audit before revision

## Conclusion

The 24-session program can support muscle growth, particularly for newer lifters, when the chosen loads genuinely meet its effort targets. It is a low-to-moderate-volume strength/Pilates/cardio hybrid, not a balanced maximum-hypertrophy prescription. Results depend on completed training, appropriate resistance, nutrition and recovery; no individual result is guaranteed.

This is an audit, not a rewrite of the sessions or automatic progression.

## Training findings

- Main lifts progress from 10–12 reps at 3 reps in reserve, through 6–8 at 2 in reserve, to 5–6 at 1–2 in reserve. These can be meaningful muscle-building sets; failure is not required.
- Two working rounds are modest volume. The code's fractional-set accounting gives the following prescribed credits per partner. Reformer credits are estimates, not validated equivalent hard sets; indirect/stabilizer credits can overstate stimulus. Power openers and deload sets are not equivalent to hard hypertrophy sets.

| Week | Chest | Back | Shoulders | Biceps | Triceps | Quads | Glutes | Hamstrings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8 | 7 | 9 | 2.5 | 6.5 | 4.5 | 6 | 3 |
| 2 | 3 | 3 | 6 | 1.5 | 2.5 | 7.5 | 12 | 7.5 |
| 3 | 6 | 6.5 | 10.5 | 3.5 | 4 | 4.5 | 6 | 3 |
| 4 | 5 | 3.5 | 5 | 1 | 3 | 9 | 12 | 6 |

- The advertised “5–9 hard sets per muscle per week” is not accurate across muscles/weeks. A blanket extra strength round does not take every muscle close to ten sets.
- Warm-up guidance uses round one of the second lift as a lighter ramp-up, while the volume calculation counts both rounds as working sets. Separate ramp-ups from the two working rounds before relying on those totals.
- Chest/back receive only one focused session in alternate weeks. Hamstring volume is low in one-lower-session weeks. Biceps have little direct work; calves have no dedicated progressive station. Shoulder totals do not prove balanced side/rear-delt work.
- A targeted knee-flexion exercise (such as a ball hamstring curl), direct biceps/calves, and more chest/back work in one-upper weeks would improve balance. Add volume gradually rather than indiscriminately adding rounds.
- Most growth-focused working sets can aim for approximately 1–3 more good reps possible. Keep power openers crisp and easier; keep the deload easier.
- Make treadmill turns genuinely easy when lifting performance is the priority; use partner pace/add recovery if breathing or fatigue prevents the intended reps. The next set of the same exercise occurs after the full station circuit, not immediately after the partner's turn.
- Weight progression currently reads only the last logged set and prescribed RIR, not achieved effort. It cannot yet establish that every working set was ready for an increase. End-of-set heart rate cannot substitute for achieved reps-in-reserve.
- Generic “add a spring” advice is inappropriate across all reformer movements: springs can add assistance/stability as well as resistance. Use exercise-specific guidance.

## Illustration findings

The exact-name correction registry is active. All 19 unique core stations resolve to 18 dedicated images; Side Plank and Hip Lift share a static pose. A still image is not a demonstration of the full movement. Spot-checked weighted dead bug, ball knee tuck, side plank and short-lever Copenhagen images show the intended equipment/support, though knee tuck trunk control and shoulder/elbow alignment deserve further refinement.

All 22 reformer stations still lack a dedicated wired reformer image. Their local fallbacks show gym/mat exercises, not the prescribed carriage/footbar/strap setups:

- Footwork and Single-Leg Press → gym leg press.
- Bridge → mat bridge; Feet-in-Straps Press-Down → leg circle; Side-Lying Press → leg lift.
- Standing Lunge → split squat; Scooter → donkey kick; Side Splits → sumo squat; Skater → curtsy lunge.
- Seated Row → bent-over row; Kneeling Lat Pull/Supine Arm Pull → gym lat pull-down.
- Long Box Pull → dumbbell supported row; T-Pull/Face Pull → generic face pull.
- Seated Chest Press → bench press; Hug a Tree → incline dumbbell press.
- Chest Expansion/Triceps Press → kickback; Biceps Curl → dumbbell curl; Overhead Triceps → dumbbell extension; Side Arm Raise → upright row.

Existing reformer contact sheets are not wired and cannot simply be labelled corrected: some subjects face the wrong direction relative to the pulleys; some panels have corrupted bodies or incorrect support positions. Review against the manual before extracting/replacing them.

Definite strength mismatches include:

- Back squat → goblet squat; dumbbell RDL → barbell RDL; swing → static RDL; squat jump → sumo squat.
- Dumbbell bench → barbell bench; seated/half-kneeling press → standing press; lateral raise → upright row; barbell overhead press → dumbbell press; push press lacks dip/drive.
- The existing bent-over-row illustration is upright/curl-like; hip-thrust illustration lacks the correct bench-supported pose and has malformed limbs; barbell bench artwork has malformed equipment.
- Reverse lunge uses a related static split-squat image, not a stepping demonstration.

For non-corrected stations the live database lookup precedes local fallbacks and permits prefix matches; the fallback audit alone cannot certify the image actually displayed in every lesson. Corrected variants must remain authoritative and exact, with equipment and support positions preserved.
